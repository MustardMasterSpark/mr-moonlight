01 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Mr. Moonlight is a slow, gritty, first-person horror game set on an Alaskan island in 1978. You are Tracey -
hungover, barefoot, and alone at a campsite where seven friends should be. This document describes Day 1: the
first of seven days, and the only one being built right now.

Title: Mr. Moonlight
Genre: First-person survival horror shooter with exploration
This document covers: The Day 1 vertical slice only
Engine: Unity 6.3 LTS
Slice platform: PC (Windows x64), released on itch.io
Later platforms: Steam and GOG, then consoles - post-slice
Audience: Adults, 18+, niche horror
Target rating: ESRB Mature 17+
Session length: 30 to 50 minutes
Team: One developer, assisted by a crew of Claude agents
Slice ship target: itch.io, first week of September 2026

THE CORE LOOP, IN ONE SENTENCE
Explore a dark forest on a fixed route, scavenge what you can carry, survive or avoid what finds you, and reach
shelter before the night closes.

WIN AND LOSS CONDITIONS
WIN: Day 1 is complete when Tracey pulls Scott out of the mine and reaches the church on the Hill of Crosses.
LOSS: Tracey dies. There is no game-over screen to manage: the level restarts at the last scene checkpoint.

ELEVATOR PITCH - THE FICTION
A group of friends, a weeklong camping trip, a remote Alaskan island. Trying to have one good last time together
before the important adulthood changes arrive. Something goes wrong.
You are Tracey, a grumpy drug addict trying to save her kidnapped friends from a cult that will sacrifice them at
the end of the week. There is something about the island: each night its terrain shifts into something deadly, and
every night it forces you to find shelter before three in the morning.
Day 1 is where none of that is known yet. Tracey wakes up with a hangover and no boots, and spends a morning
being annoyed about it. By nightfall she is dragging an unconscious friend through a mine while people in black
whistle to each other in the dark.

ELEVATOR PITCH - THE MECHANICS OF THE SLICE
- A fixed route, hand built. Day 1 runs on one authored path through eight locations. The procedural island
  generator that reshuffles the map every day is a full-game feature and is not in the slice - see section 12.
- Navigation you have to stop for. A map and a compass, taken from William's tent. Deploying them locks Tracey
  in place and exposes her, so reading the map is a decision, not a free action.
- Combat that costs something. A pickaxe with heavy slow swings, and two firearms handed over late. Every blow
  drains stamina. Some encounters are meant to be walked around, not won.
- Fear as a visible state. Fear rises at scripted moments and shows itself through chromatic aberration, camera
  shake and Tracey's breathing. The player reads their own condition off the screen and the headphones, not off
  a number.
- A backpack that is too small. The coolers at the campsite hold more than Tracey can carry. The fifth pickup is
  refused, and the player has to drop something.
- Sound as information. A radio call locates a person. A whistle locates a patrol. Sprinting makes noise, which is
  why the boots matter.

COMPARABLE GAMES

Misery (2025) - A daily run against a hard clock, ending at a shelter.
Dusk (2018) - Crude, violent low-poly shooting against a cult.
The Forest (2018) - Resource pressure and a hostile population in the woods.
Silent Hill 1 (1999) - Plain white subtitle lines, and telling the story through them rather than around them.

02 STORY

1978. AANNIARVIK ISLAND, ALASKA.
Seven friends are arriving at the point in life where the important decisions get made, and they are all quietly
afraid that this is where the friendship ends. So they take a break from the hard Alaskan winter routine and go to
an island for one last weeklong camping trip, with booze and enough other things to make it memorable.
Aanniarvik was inhabited by natives a long time ago. During the Second World War it became an American base,
waiting for a Japanese invasion that never came. Now it is an empty island with a population of one: a forest
ranger, the uncle of Holly, your childhood friend.
You are Tracey. Twenty-something, a history major who dropped out, in possession of some nasty habits and a
permanently foul mouth. You did not want to come. Your friends insisted far too much. Fine. One more time.

03 CAST

Five characters exist in Day 1. Three more are named in dialogue but never appear.

Tracey - PLAYABLE. Addict, asshole, dull - her words. The player never sees her face, only her hands.
Rylee - VOICE ON THE RADIO. Lady jester. Has Tracey's boots, falling asleep while talking.
Holly - AT THE CABIN. Miss Perfect. Hands over the marijuana.
Scott - FOUND IN THE MINE. Transmits once on the radio, then an unconscious body on a stretcher.
Vernon - AT THE CABIN, THEN ON THE RADIO. Holly's uncle. Gives Tracey the M1911 and the morphine.

NAMED BUT NOT PRESENT IN DAY 1
Robert, William and Shannon are talked about in Rylee's radio conversation and appear in the Polaroid photograph.
Amaruq and Mr. Moonlight are named in the full-game fiction and do not appear anywhere in the Day 1 script.

04 GAME FLOW

The slice is one day, not seven. Loop: EXPLORE (map + compass, stamina, light, sound) -> SCAVENGE (pick up
items, backpack is capped) -> ENCOUNTER (wolves, cultists, bear trap; fight it or slip past it) -> OBJECTIVE
ADVANCES (pause menu updates) -> next scene. On death/capture: restart at the last scene checkpoint (automatic,
no save menu). After objectives 1-6 close: NIGHT FALLS after the mine -> REACH THE CHURCH -> DAY 1 COMPLETE.

WHAT CHANGED FROM VERSION 0.1
Structure: seven days each with its own objective -> one day, seven objectives inside it.
Saving: limited-use manual save system -> removed, automatic checkpoint at the start of each scene.
Map: regenerated procedurally every day -> one fixed, hand-built route.
Night deadline: reach shelter before 3:00 a.m. or die -> night arrives on script after the mine, not a live mechanic.
Failure: return to the beginning of the day -> return to the start of the current scene.

05 THE ISLAND IN DAY 1

The player moves east from the campsite to the Glade, then to Vernon's cabin, then into the mine, then north to
the church. No optional areas, no backtracking. The only branch is at the bear trap.

LOCATIONS
Camping site (Scenes 01-04) - RV, two tents, William's locked tent, bonfire, canteen table, two coolers.
The forest road (Scene 05) - A single wolf crosses at distance and ignores Tracey. Fear is introduced here.
The Glade (Scene 06) - Observation post with mounted telescope. Boots, Polaroid, pickaxe, a light in the distance.
Vernon's cabin (Scenes 07-08) - The only safe room in the level. Holly and Vernon are here.
Flak tower (Scene 08) - Visible in the distance, cannot be entered.
Mineshaft (Scene 09) - Linear tunnel, entered with a lamp. Cultists inside. Infirmary holds Scott.
The church (Scene 10) - Reached after the Virgin Mary statue and up the Hill of Crosses, Furman in pursuit.

TWO LOCATIONS WERE CUT: The Dock and the Radio station / Workshop.

06 LOOK AND FEEL

07 GAME MECHANICS

Everything below exists in the Day 1 script. Nothing here is aspirational: if a system is listed, at least one
event line in L01.txt requires it.

7.1 WHAT THE PLAYER DOES

Verb: Look | What it means: Free camera. Blocked during scripted moments. | When it unlocks: After the first
dialogue lines
Verb: Move | What it means: Walk. Slower while holding the radio. | When it unlocks: After the vomit animation
Verb: Sprint | What it means: Faster, and noticeably louder than walking. | When it unlocks: When the boots go
on, at the Glade
Verb: Interact | What it means: One button. Picks up, opens, pulls, knocks. | When it unlocks: At the wristwatch,
as the first tutorial
Verb: Carry and drop | What it means: Backpack holds a limited number of items; the player chooses what to
lose. | When it unlocks: At the blue cooler
Verb: Navigate | What it means: Deploy map and compass. Locks Tracey in place while open. | When it unlocks:
From William's tent
Verb: Swing | What it means: Heavy, slow pickaxe blows that drain stamina. | When it unlocks: Pulled from the
telescope frame
Verb: Shoot | What it means: M1911, then the Arisaka. Loud, scarce, and a last resort. | When it unlocks: Vernon's
cabin, then the mine infirmary
Verb: Drag | What it means: Pull Scott on the stretcher. Movement and combat are restricted. | When it unlocks:
The mine infirmary

7.2 SYSTEMS IN THE SLICE

Twenty systems. The last one is the keystone - without it nothing else runs in sequence.

System: Player controller | What the player sees: Walking, looking, sprinting, first-person arms. | What it has
to hold: Speed, sprint state, noise level. | Built from: Owned FPS asset, adapted

System: Control locks | What the player sees: The camera or the legs stop answering during a scripted beat. |
What it has to hold: Two independent boolean locks that always get released. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Interaction | What the player sees: A prompt icon appears when something can be used. | What it has to
hold: Range, line of sight, required item. | Built from: Owned FPS asset

System: Inventory | What the player sees: The fifth pickup is refused and Tracey complains. | What it has to
hold: Capacity, contents, discard. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Stamina | What the player sees: A bar that appears for the first time during the vomiting, then drains
and refills. | What it has to hold: Current, max, drain and regen rates. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Health and damage | What the player sees: Getting bitten hurts and does not come back on its own. |
What it has to hold: Current, max, damage sources. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Fear | What the player sees: Chromatic aberration, camera shake, and breathing that gets heavier. | What
it has to hold: A single fear value with thresholds that drive VFX and audio. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Melee | What the player sees: Slow pickaxe swings that cost stamina and stagger a Furman. | What it has
to hold: Damage, swing time, stamina cost, stun. | Built from: Owned FPS asset, extended

System: Firearms | What the player sees: Two guns, arriving late, loud enough to matter. | What it has to hold:
Damage, fire rate, ammo count. | Built from: Owned FPS asset

System: Map and compass | What the player sees: The screen fills with a paper map and Tracey stops moving. |
What it has to hold: Player heading, position, deploy lock. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Light | What the player sees: A mining lamp in the tunnel, matches as an emergency. | What it has to
hold: Range, intensity, fuel or battery. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Substances | What the player sees: Morphine and marijuana are handed over and can be used once. | What
it has to hold: Instant buff, timed drawback. | Built from: Systems Smith, minimal version

System: Bear trap | What the player sees: A snap, a large chunk of health and stamina gone, and a limp. | What it
has to hold: Trigger volume, damage, injured state. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Stretcher escort | What the player sees: Scott comes with you, slowly, and you cannot fight properly. |
What it has to hold: Attach point, speed penalty, restrictions. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Objective tracker | What the player sees: A line in the pause menu that changes as the day goes wrong. |
What it has to hold: Start, update and end for seven objectives. | Built from: Level Weaver

System: Subtitles | What the player sees: Plain white lines, typewriter or immediate. | What it has to hold: Text,
colour, display method, onscreen time. | Built from: Nani Novel, adapted

System: Audio manager | What the player sees: Ambience, prop sources, enemy signatures, breathing. | What it has
to hold: Mixer states driven by fear and location. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Scene checkpoint | What the player sees: Dying puts you back at the start of the current scene. | What it
has to hold: Current scene index and its entry state. | Built from: Systems Smith

System: Time of day | What the player sees: Morning at the camp, dusk at the cabin, full dark at the mine exit. |
What it has to hold: A lighting set per scene. Not a live clock. | Built from: Baked lightmaps

System: SLDD runner | What the player sees: Nothing directly - it is why every other thing happens in order. |
What it has to hold: Parses L01.txt and fires each event in sequence. | Built from: Level Weaver

7.3 HOW THE LEVEL SCRIPT DRIVES THE ENGINE

Day 1 is not scripted inside Unity. It is written in a plain-text format of my own, the Script Level Design
Document, as 223 numbered event lines. Each line is one event, with a fixed type and a fixed parameter list,
separated by double pipes. Example:

L01E-039 || PFX || Name: Stamina refill || Description: Refill part of the stamina bar and begin slow stamina
recovery. || Stat: N/A || Magnitude: N/A || Duration: N/A || Stacking: F || Curve: N/A

Each event type maps to one implementation (PA, SA, D:@/T:@/SM, HA, WA/CA:@/EA:@, CB:B/MB:B, C, VFX:UI,
VFX:PV, SFX, PFX, O:#:S/U/E, OL). PFX = Player effect: a stat modifier applied to the player stats block, built by
Systems Smith.

NOTE: The current script file in a txt engine will be obfuscated and stored within Unity run files.

08 ENEMIES AND THREATS

Wolf (E-W) - Introduced at distance on the forest road, ignores Tracey. Returns as a pack at the Glade.
Behaviour: Roam, circle the player, bite, break off when struck, flee when hurt, afraid of fire.

Cultist Zealot (E-Z) - Branch A of the road to the cabin. Must be winnable with the pickaxe and nothing else.
Behaviour: Hidden, charge on sight, melee, follow at distance if the player escapes, low health.

Cultist Spotter (E-S) - Branch B. Patrol with a lamp, ignore at distance, whistle to summon, never lose interest
once alerted. Fighting them while injured is meant to kill you.

Furman (E-F) - Appears once, in the night escape, pursuing Tracey up the Hill of Crosses. A chase, not an
encounter. Behaviour: Charge, heavy bites, raises fear on approach, large health pool, staggered by a heavy blow.

Bear trap - One trap, on the road to the cabin. Cannot kill, but takes a large amount of health and stamina and
decides which of the two branches the player gets. Behaviour: Trigger volume, one-shot, applies the injured state,
gates the branch.

CUT FROM THE ENEMY ROSTER: Stone totems, the Cultist Priest and punji traps.

10 AI ARCHITECTURE

Eight agents, all development tools; none ships inside the game. Script Warden, Slice Producer, Level Weaver,
Systems Smith, Enemy Brain, QA Hunter, Package Cartographer, Scribe. They share four flat files: script-defects.md,
today.md, package-map.md, build-report.md.

RULES THE AGENTS WORK UNDER
1. Nothing gets invented. If a needed detail is not in the three source documents, the agent marks it TBD and stops.
2. The format specification wins on format, the level script wins on Day 1 content, this document wins on everything else.
3. Purchased packages are wrapped, not rewritten. Adapters go in our own namespace.
4. One merged change per system per day.
5. Every change arrives with the event IDs it implements.
